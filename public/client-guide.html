What Is This Website?
Section 1.1
The Big Picture — Two Websites in One

Your website is actually two things working together as one system. Think of it like a shop that has a front part where customers walk in and browse, and a back office where you and your team manage everything. Both parts live at the same address on the internet, but different people see different things.

🛒

The Public Website

This is what your customers see when they visit your website address. Anyone in the world can see this part — no login needed. They can browse your farm products, read about your company, check prices, and place orders.

🔒

The Admin Panel

This is the private control room that only you and your trusted team can access. You log in at /admin and from here you manage products, view orders, pay workers, send emails, track money — everything.

The website was built specifically for T-Akomz Agro Estates Ltd, an agricultural business in Ido Ekiti, Ekiti State. It sells farm products like poultry, eggs, livestock, crop produce, dairy, and organic inputs directly to customers online. Customers can pay with their card, bank transfer, or request cash on delivery.

Section 1.2
What You Can Control

As the business owner or manager, you have complete control over everything. Nothing on the website is fixed or permanent — you can change it whenever you want. Here is a full list of what you can do from your admin panel:

Everything You Can Manage

  • Add, edit, or remove any product and its price
  • Upload product photos (multiple photos per product)
  • Track how many items are in stock
  • View every order placed by every customer
  • Change an order's status (e.g., mark it as delivered)
  • Download PDF invoices for any order
  • See a list of all customers and their history
  • Add and manage your farm workers' records
  • Run payroll — record and track salary payments
  • Track business income and expenses
  • Send email messages to all customers or individual ones
  • Send email messages to all workers or staff members
  • Write and publish blog/news articles
  • Change your business contact information
  • Use AI tools to write product descriptions and business plans
  • View sales reports and revenue charts
Section 1.3
User Roles — Who Can Do What

Not everyone who logs in sees the same things. The website has a role system — like giving different keys to different people. Here are the roles:

RoleWho Has ItWhat They Can Do
SUPER_ADMIN The business owner Everything — full access to all features including settings, financial records, user management, and the download guide button on the dashboard.
ADMIN Senior manager Full access to the admin panel except changing site settings and managing other admin users.
MANAGER Department manager Can access the admin panel, submit expense requests, record direct expenses (with approval), manage workers and payroll.
SALES Sales team member Can view orders, manage products, and use communications tools.
CUSTOMER Online shoppers Can only see the public website, their own orders, wishlist, and account. Cannot access the admin panel at all.
Important to Know

Every person who creates an account on your website starts as a CUSTOMER automatically. Only you (as SUPER_ADMIN) can upgrade someone to a staff role like MANAGER or SALES. This means there is zero risk of a random customer accidentally accessing your admin panel.

The Public Website
Section 2.1
The Homepage

The homepage is the very first page a visitor sees when they type your website address into their browser. It is designed to make a strong first impression — dark, premium, and professional. Think of it as your digital shop front.

Here is everything that appears on the homepage, from top to bottom:

  1. Navigation Bar (Navbar) — The bar at the very top of every page. It shows your logo on the left, page links in the middle (Home, Shop, About, Blog, Contact), and on the right: a search icon, a heart icon for the wishlist, a shopping cart icon with a number showing how many items are inside, and a login button. On mobile phones, the links collapse into a menu icon (three horizontal lines).
  2. Hero Section — The very first big section you see when you land on the page. It fills the entire screen. It has a large headline, a short description of your business, and two buttons: "Shop Now" (takes them to the shop) and "Learn More" (scrolls down). There is also a live animated stat counter showing business numbers.
  3. Featured Products — A row of product cards showing your best or newest products so customers can start shopping immediately without even going to the shop page.
  4. How It Works — A simple step-by-step section explaining to customers how easy it is to order from you: Browse → Add to Cart → Pay → Receive Delivery.
  5. About Section — A brief introduction to T-Akomz Agro Estates, your mission, and why customers should trust your products.
  6. Testimonials / Reviews — Customer reviews and ratings shown to build confidence in new visitors.
  7. Newsletter Signup — A box where visitors can type their email address to subscribe to your newsletter. Whenever you release a new product, they get notified by email automatically.
  8. Footer — At the very bottom of every page. Shows your contact details, business address (Oke-Ido Road, Ido Ekiti, Ekiti State), links to important pages, and social media links.
Section 2.2
The Shop Page

The shop page is where all your products are displayed. When a customer clicks "Shop" in the navbar, this is where they land.

The shop page has several helpful features:

🔍

Search

Customers can type a product name (like "eggs" or "chicken") and the shop instantly filters to show only matching products.

📂

Category Filter

Customers can click a category (Poultry, Eggs, Livestock, etc.) to see only products in that group. Makes it much easier to find what they want.

↕️

Sort Options

Customers can sort products by price (low to high or high to low), by newest first, or by most popular.

🃏

Product Cards

Each product is shown as a card with its photo, name, price, star rating, and an "Add to Cart" button. Clicking the card opens the full product page.

Your product categories are: Poultry (chicken, turkey, duck), Eggs (table eggs, fertilized, quail), Livestock (goat, sheep, pig), Crop Produce (maize, cassava, yam, vegetables), Dairy (milk, yogurt), Organic Inputs (compost, seedlings), and Farm Subscription Boxes (weekly delivery).

Section 2.3
The Product Page

When a customer clicks on any product card, they are taken to that product's full detail page. This page shows them everything they need to know before buying.

What is on a product page:

  • Product Photos — A large main photo, and smaller thumbnail photos below it. The customer can click a thumbnail to see that photo bigger. All photos are uploaded by you from the admin panel.
  • Product Name and Price — The name in large text and the price in Nigerian Naira (₦) format.
  • Star Rating — The average star rating from all customer reviews (out of 5 stars).
  • Description — A full description of the product. What it is, how it was raised/grown, why it is good quality. You write this when you create the product, or you can use the AI tool to write it for you.
  • Nutrition Facts — If the product has nutrition information (like protein, fat, calories), it is displayed in a neat table. You enter this data when creating the product.
  • Weight / Unit — The weight or unit size of the product (e.g., "1 kg", "1 dozen", "1 carton").
  • Quantity Selector — The customer can increase or decrease the number they want to buy using + and − buttons.
  • Add to Cart Button — The main green button. Clicking it puts the selected quantity into the shopping cart.
  • Add to Wishlist (Heart Icon) — If the customer is logged in, they can save the product to their wishlist for later by clicking the heart icon.
  • Stock Status — If an item is in stock, it shows "In Stock." If the stock is low (below your warning level), it shows "Only X left!" in yellow to create urgency. If completely out of stock, the Add to Cart button is disabled.
  • Customer Reviews Section — Below the product info, all verified customer reviews are shown. Each review has a star rating, the customer's name, the date they left the review, and their written comment. If a customer has never bought the product, they cannot leave a review.
  • Related Products — At the very bottom, the website automatically suggests similar products the customer might also want.
Section 2.4
The Shopping Cart

The cart is where a customer stores products they want to buy before they actually pay. Think of it exactly like a real shopping basket in a supermarket — they pick things up, put them in the basket, and then go to the till (checkout) when they are ready.

Your cart has a very important feature: it works for both guests and logged-in customers, and it remembers their items.

👤

Guest Cart (Not Logged In)

Even if someone has not created an account, they can still add products to the cart. The items are saved in their browser session. If they leave the page, the items stay until they close the browser tab.

✅

Logged-In Cart

When a customer is logged in, their cart is saved in the database. This means even if they close their laptop and come back a week later, their cart items are still there.

Cart Merge — Smart Feature

If a guest adds items to their cart and THEN logs in, the website automatically combines the guest cart with their saved account cart. Nothing is lost. This is completely automatic.

The cart page itself shows:

  • Every product in the cart with its photo, name, and price per unit
  • A quantity field for each item — the customer can type a new number or use the +/− buttons
  • A "Remove" button to delete an item from the cart
  • A subtotal for each line (price × quantity)
  • An order summary box showing subtotal, delivery fee, any coupon discount, and the grand total
  • A coupon code field — if the customer has a discount code, they type it here
  • A "Proceed to Checkout" button to go and pay

The cart icon in the navigation bar shows a small green number badge that updates in real time as items are added or removed — so the customer always knows how many items they have without opening the cart.

Section 2.5
The Checkout Process — Step by Step

Checkout is the process of actually paying for the items in the cart. The website guides the customer through this in clear steps — they cannot skip ahead and they cannot get confused about what to do next.

  1. Step 1 — Delivery Information
    The customer fills in where they want the order delivered. They enter their full name, phone number, delivery address (street, city, state), and any special delivery instructions. If they have saved addresses from previous orders, they can just select one from a dropdown instead of typing it all again.
  2. Step 2 — Order Review
    The customer sees a complete summary of everything they are buying: each product, the quantity, the price per item, the subtotal, any delivery fee, any coupon discount, and the final total in Nigerian Naira (₦). This is their last chance to check everything before paying. They can go back and change quantities or remove items.
  3. Step 3 — Payment
    The customer chooses how they want to pay. There are three options (explained in detail in the next section). Once they click the final "Pay Now" button, the system creates their order with a unique order number in the format TAK-2025-00001 and processes the payment.
Order Numbers

Every order gets a unique reference number in this exact format: TAK-YYYY-NNNNN. For example, TAK-2025-00047 means the 47th order placed in the year 2025. Both you and the customer can use this number to find the order instantly.

Section 2.6
Payment Methods — Explained in Full

Your website accepts three different ways to pay. Each one is explained below in complete detail.

Option A — Paystack (Main Payment Method)

Paystack is the most popular payment company in Nigeria. It is trusted by thousands of Nigerian businesses and is completely safe. Your customers can pay with their Nigerian debit card, bank transfer, USSD code, or even mobile money — all through Paystack's secure page.

  1. Customer clicks "Pay with Paystack" — They are taken away from your website and to Paystack's own secure payment page (you will see the padlock icon in the browser and the web address will start with paystack.com).
  2. Customer enters card details — They type their 16-digit card number, expiry date, and CVV (the 3-digit code on the back of the card). Alternatively, they can choose bank transfer or USSD — Paystack will show them the bank account to transfer to.
  3. Customer enters OTP — Their bank sends a one-time password (OTP) to their registered phone number. They type this code to authorize the payment.
  4. Paystack processes the payment — This takes about 3–5 seconds. The customer sees a loading spinner.
  5. Paystack notifies your website — As soon as the payment goes through, Paystack automatically sends a secret notification (called a "webhook") to your website. This happens in the background, even if the customer closes their browser. Your website receives this notification and immediately marks the order as PAID and CONFIRMED.
  6. Customer is redirected back — The customer lands on your Order Success page. They see their order number, a summary of what they bought, and a confirmation message.
  7. Confirmation email is sent automatically — Within seconds, the customer receives an email with their full order receipt, including a PDF invoice attached to the email.
Money Goes Directly to You

Money paid via Paystack goes directly to your Paystack business account. From there, you can withdraw it to your bank account at any time through the Paystack dashboard. Your website never holds or stores any money — it just facilitates the transaction.

Option B — Flutterwave (Alternative Payment)

Flutterwave is another highly trusted Nigerian payment company. It works almost identically to Paystack. The process is the same — the customer is taken to Flutterwave's secure page, they pay, and Flutterwave notifies your website when done. Your website then marks the order as paid and sends the confirmation email.

Flutterwave supports: debit/credit cards, bank transfers, USSD, mobile money, and even payments from other African countries (Ghana, Kenya, etc.). Having two payment options means if one is having technical problems, customers can always use the other one.

Option C — Cash on Delivery

This option is for customers who prefer to pay in cash when the delivery arrives at their door. They do NOT pay anything online.

  1. Customer selects "Pay on Delivery" — They confirm their order without entering any card details.
  2. Order is created as PENDING / UNPAID — The system creates the order with payment status "UNPAID." You can see it in your admin panel immediately.
  3. Your team contacts the customer — You (or your delivery team) call the customer to confirm the order and arrange delivery.
  4. Delivery is made and cash is collected — Your delivery person collects the cash at the customer's door.
  5. Admin updates the order — You go into the admin panel, find the order, and manually mark it as PAID and DELIVERED.
Cash on Delivery Caution

Cash on delivery carries some risk — a customer might order and then not be available when delivery arrives. It is a good idea to call and confirm the order before dispatching your delivery person. You can choose to disable this payment option in your site settings if you prefer card-only payments.

Section 2.7
After Payment — What the Customer Sees

After a successful payment, the customer lands on the Order Success page. This page is very important — it reassures the customer that everything went through correctly.

The Order Success page shows:

  • A large green checkmark and "Order Confirmed!" message
  • The order number (e.g., TAK-2025-00047) — they should save or screenshot this
  • A summary of what they ordered and the total they paid
  • The delivery address they entered
  • An estimated delivery timeframe
  • A button to "Track Your Order" which takes them to their account order history
  • A button to "Continue Shopping"

At the exact same moment, the customer also receives an automatic email to their email address. This email contains their full receipt and a PDF invoice attached — so they have a permanent record. You also receive a copy in your admin panel.

Customer Accounts
Section 3.1
Registering and Logging In

Customers do not have to create an account to browse your shop or even to check out. However, having an account gives them useful features like order history, saved addresses, and a wishlist. Creating an account is free and takes about 30 seconds.

Creating a New Account

  1. Customer clicks "Sign Up" or "Register" in the navbar.
  2. They fill in their full name, email address, and a password (must be at least 8 characters). They type the password twice to confirm they did not make a typo.
  3. They click the "Create Account" button.
  4. The system creates their account instantly and logs them in automatically — no waiting for an email verification.
  5. A Welcome email is sent automatically to their email address. This email greets them by name, explains what they can do with their account, and shows your contact details.

Logging In (Returning Customers)

  1. Customer clicks "Login" in the navbar.
  2. They type their email address and password.
  3. If they tick "Remember me", the website remembers them for 30 days — they will not need to log in again on that device.
  4. They click "Log In" and are taken directly to their account dashboard.

Forgot Password

If a customer forgets their password, they click "Forgot your password?" on the login page. They enter their email address and the system sends them a reset link. They click the link in the email, enter a new password, and they are back in. The reset link expires after 60 minutes for security.

Section 3.4
Wishlist

The wishlist is like a "save for later" list. A logged-in customer can click the heart icon on any product and it gets added to their wishlist. They can come back later to buy it.

The heart icon appears on product cards in the shop and on the full product page. When a product is already in the wishlist, the heart turns solid (filled in). When they click it again, it is removed from the wishlist.

In the navigation bar, there is a small heart icon. If the customer has any items in their wishlist, a green number badge appears on this icon showing how many items are saved. Clicking the heart icon in the navbar takes them straight to their full wishlist page.

On the wishlist page, the customer can:

  • See all their saved products with photos, names, and current prices
  • Add any wishlist item directly to their cart
  • Remove items from the wishlist they no longer want
Section 3.5
Saved Addresses

Customers can save their delivery addresses so they do not have to type the full address every time they order. This is especially useful for regular customers.

A customer can save multiple addresses — for example, their home address and their office address. During checkout, they simply select the address from a dropdown instead of filling in the form.

To manage addresses, the customer goes to "My Account → Addresses." From there they can:

  • Add a new address by filling in the address form
  • Edit any existing address (e.g., if they moved)
  • Delete an address they no longer need
  • Set one address as the "Default" — this one is pre-selected automatically at checkout
Section 3.6
Profile and Password

Under "My Account → Profile," a customer can update their personal information.

What they can change:

  • Name — Update their display name (this appears on their reviews and account)
  • Email address — Change the email they use to log in and receive emails from you
  • Phone number — Add or update their phone number
  • Profile photo — Upload a personal photo (shown in their account and in the "New Customers" section of your admin dashboard)
  • Password — Change their password. They must enter their current password first (for security), then the new password twice.
Security Note

To change a password, the customer MUST know their current password. This prevents anyone else from changing their password even if they get access to the account temporarily. If they have forgotten their current password, they must use the "Forgot Password" flow instead.

The Admin Panel
Section 4.1
How to Access the Admin Panel

The admin panel is your private control room. No customer can ever see it or access it — it is completely locked behind a login system and role check.

To get to the admin panel:

  1. Open your web browser and go to your website address followed by /admin. For example: yoursite.com/admin
  2. You will see the login page. Type your admin email address and password.
  3. Click "Log In". The system checks your role — if you are not an ADMIN or SUPER_ADMIN, access is denied immediately.
  4. If approved, you land on the Admin Dashboard.
Never Share Your Admin Password

Your admin login is the "master key" to your entire business data. Never share it. If a staff member needs access, create them a separate account with the appropriate role — do not share your own login credentials.

The admin panel has a dark-themed sidebar on the left side of the screen. This sidebar contains links to every section of the admin panel, organized into groups. At the top of the sidebar, you can see your name and role badge. The sidebar is always visible while you work, making it easy to jump between sections.

Section 4.2
The Admin Dashboard — Understanding Every Number

When you first log into the admin panel, you land on the Dashboard. This page gives you a live snapshot of how your business is performing right now. Everything on this page updates automatically — you do not need to refresh it manually.

The 4 KPI Cards (Top Row)

KPI stands for "Key Performance Indicator" — these are the most important numbers in your business. The four cards at the top of the dashboard show:

Revenue Chart (Bar Chart)

Below the KPI cards is a bar chart showing your revenue for the last 12 months. Each bar represents one month. The taller the bar, the more money you earned that month. You can hover your mouse over any bar to see the exact Naira amount for that month. This helps you spot seasonal patterns — for example, if your sales spike around Christmas or the farming season.

Order Status Breakdown

Next to the chart is a breakdown showing how many orders are in each status (PENDING, CONFIRMED, DELIVERED, CANCELLED, etc.) with progress bars showing the percentage. This tells you at a glance if there are too many PENDING orders piling up that need your attention.

Top Products

This section shows your 5 best-selling products by revenue — which products have made you the most money. Each product is numbered, shows the units sold, and the total revenue it has generated. Use this to know which products to keep in stock and promote more.

Low Stock Alert

If any of your products are running low on stock (below the warning threshold — default is 10 units), they appear here with the exact number remaining in yellow text. This is your early warning system so you never accidentally sell out of something and have to cancel customer orders.

New Customers

The latest customers who created accounts are shown here with their profile photo and how long ago they joined. A quick way to see who is new to your shop.

Recent Orders Table

At the bottom of the dashboard is a table showing the 10 most recent orders. For each order you can see the order number, customer name, total amount, payment status, order status, and a "View" link that takes you directly to the full order detail. This means you can act on new orders without even leaving the dashboard.

Section 5.2
Adding Product Photos

Photos are extremely important — customers cannot touch or smell the product online, so good photos are how they judge quality. You can upload multiple photos per product.

  1. After saving the product, you are taken to the product's edit page (or you can find any product under Admin → Products and click "Edit").
  2. Scroll to the "Product Images" section. You will see an upload area.
  3. Click the upload area or drag your photo files onto it. You can select multiple photos at once.
  4. The photos upload automatically and appear as thumbnails on the page.
  5. You can drag the thumbnails to reorder them — the first photo is the "main photo" that customers see first.
  6. To remove a photo, click the X button on its thumbnail.

Photo Tips

  • Use good lighting — natural daylight photos look best
  • Show the product from multiple angles
  • Include a photo with a scale reference so customers understand the size
  • Minimum recommended photo size: 800×800 pixels
  • The website automatically compresses and resizes photos — you do not need to edit them before uploading
Section 5.3
Editing and Deleting Products

To see all your products, go to Admin → Products. You will see a table with all products, their current price, stock level, status, and action buttons.

To edit a product (change the price, update the description, change the stock level, etc.):

  1. Find the product in the list. You can search by name or filter by category.
  2. Click the "Edit" button (pencil icon) on that product's row.
  3. The same form you used to create the product opens, pre-filled with all the current values.
  4. Change whatever you need to change.
  5. Click "Update Product." The changes appear on your website immediately.

To delete a product:

  • Click the red "Delete" button on the product row.
  • A confirmation box appears asking "Are you sure?" — click Yes to permanently delete it.
  • Deleted products cannot be recovered. If you just want to hide a product temporarily, set its status to "Draft" or "Inactive" instead of deleting it.
Think Before Deleting

If you delete a product that has existing orders, those orders will still show the product name in their history (the order history is preserved). But the product will no longer be searchable or shoppable on your website. If you are unsure, set the product to "Inactive" status instead — it disappears from the shop but all your historical data stays intact.

Section 5.4
Managing Categories

Categories are the groups that your products are organized into. Go to Admin → Categories to manage them.

You can:

  • Add a new category — Give it a name and optionally a description and an icon. Example: "Poultry" with a chicken emoji as the icon.
  • Edit a category — Change the name or description.

Section 8.2
Expense Management — The Approval Workflow

The expense system has a structured workflow with role-based controls. This prevents any single person from spending business money without proper oversight.

How the Expense Approval Workflow Works

  1. Manager Submits an Expense Request
    A MANAGER goes to Admin → Finance → Expenses and clicks "New Expense Request." They fill in the description (what the money is for), the amount, the category (e.g., Feed Purchase, Transport, Utilities), and optionally upload a receipt photo. They submit the request.

    The moment they submit, an automatic email is sent to all ADMIN and SUPER_ADMIN users alerting them that a new expense request is waiting for approval.
  2. Admin Reviews the Request
    You (or another admin) log into the admin panel and see the pending expense request under Admin → Finance → Expenses → Pending. You can see all the details the manager submitted, including any receipt they uploaded.
  3. Admin Approves or Rejects
    You click either "Approve" or "Reject". If rejecting, you can type a reason.

    The moment you decide, an automatic email is sent to the manager telling them their request was approved or rejected — and if rejected, the reason you gave.
  4. Approved Expense is Recorded
    If approved, the expense is automatically added to the financial ledger, reducing the wallet balance by that amount. The transaction appears in the full ledger with a note showing it was approved by you.

ADMIN and SUPER_ADMIN can also record expenses directly — without going through the approval process. This is for situations where you yourself are making a business purchase and you want to record it immediately.

When a manager records a direct expense (if they have been granted that permission), an automatic email is sent to all admins alerting them of the direct expense — so there is always transparency even without a formal approval step.

Section 10.5
Security — How Your Website Is Protected

Security has been built into every part of your website. Here is what is protecting you and your customers:

ProtectionWhat It Does
HTTPS / SSLAll data between the user's browser and your server is encrypted. No one can read passwords, card details, or personal information in transit. The padlock icon in the browser confirms this.
CSRF ProtectionEvery form on your website has a hidden security token. This prevents attackers from tricking users into submitting forms without their knowledge (Cross-Site Request Forgery attacks).
Role-Based AccessEvery admin page checks your role before showing you anything. Even if someone guesses the URL to an admin page, they are denied access without the right role.
Payment Webhook VerificationWhen Paystack or Flutterwave notifies your website of a payment, the website verifies the notification is genuinely from Paystack/Flutterwave using a cryptographic signature. Fake payment notifications are rejected.
Private File StorageFinancial receipts, sensitive documents, and uploaded files are stored in a private area that is not accessible via the public internet. Only authenticated users can access them through secure routes.
Checkout IDOR ProtectionA customer can only see their own order confirmation page — they cannot view another customer's order by guessing the order number in the URL.
Input ValidationEvery form validates data before saving it. Wrong data types, too-short inputs, invalid email formats — all rejected with a helpful error message.
Password HashingPasswords are never stored as plain text. They are converted to an unreadable hash. Even if someone stole the database, they could not read any passwords.
Address Ownership CheckA customer can only edit and delete their own saved addresses. They cannot modify another customer's addresses even if they know the ID.
Your Customers' Data Is Safe

Your website does not store any card numbers or payment details. All payment information is handled entirely by Paystack or Flutterwave on their own secure servers. Your website only receives confirmation that a payment was successful — not the actual card data. This means even if your website was compromised, no card data could be stolen from it.

A Note for Your Web Developer

This section covers the technical commands your developer needs to maintain the website. It is included for completeness but is not expected to be understood by non-technical staff.

Key Technical Commands

  • php artisan serve — Starts the Laravel development server on your local machine
  • npm run dev — Starts the Vite build tool for CSS/JavaScript hot reload during development
  • php artisan migrate — Runs any pending database schema changes
  • php artisan migrate:fresh --seed — Completely rebuilds the database from scratch (DANGER — wipes all data — development only)

  • php artisan queue:work — Starts processing background jobs (email sending, notifications). Must be running in production.
